# Photography and filming — DRAFT

> **STATUS: DRAFT FOR REVIEW.** Covers the policy, the consent wording already in the
> sign-up form, and the practical problem of honouring it on the day.

## Policy

Photography and filming are **opt-in**. Nobody is photographed for promotional use without
permission, and refusing has no effect on anyone's place in the competition.

For a player under 18, **the parent or guardian gives this permission, not the player.**
A child cannot consent to their own image being published. This is enforced in the form:
the photo consent tick sits in the guardian section and is separate from the tick that
permits entry, so it is a genuinely free choice rather than something bundled into "yes I
want to play".

The default is no. An absent answer means no.

## What consent covers, and what it does not

Consent is for: photographs and video taken at the event, used on the SWC website, SWC
social media accounts, and in material promoting future SWC events.

Consent is **not** for: sale or licensing to third parties, use by sponsors in their own
advertising, press distribution, or anything implying endorsement.
`[If you want sponsor or press use, that is a separate tick with its own wording — do not
stretch this one to cover it.]`

Consent can be withdrawn at any time by emailing `[EMAIL]`. We will remove the image from
anywhere we control within `[14]` days. We cannot un-publish something already reshared by
someone else, and the consent wording should say so honestly.

## Honouring it on the day — the hard part

A policy that only exists in a database is not a policy. On the day, a photographer with a
long lens has no idea who consented.

**Needed:**

- `[A visible marker for non-consenting players — coloured wristbands are the standard
  answer, and the marker should mark the NON-consenting group only if that is the smaller
  set, or the consenting group if not. Either way the photographer's rule must be a single
  simple sentence.]`
- A printed list at the check-in desk, and a briefing for every photographer before they
  start.
- One named person the photographers can ask.
- No photography in `[toilets, changing areas, quiet rooms, prayer spaces]`.
- Group and crowd shots: `[decide — either treat as covered by the same consent, or avoid
  identifiable crowd shots entirely. The second is safer and cheaper to enforce.]`

## Storage

Event photography is currently the largest undocumented store of children's personal data
in the project — it lives entirely outside the app. It needs an owner, a location, an access
list and a retention period (`RETENTION-POLICY.md` proposes `[3]` years).

Photographs must not remain on a photographer's personal device or card after the event.

## The wording in the form today

Guardian-facing, for under-18s:

> "I'm happy for my child to appear in photos and video from the day."
> *Completely optional, and it never affects their place. If you leave it, our
> photographers are told and they won't be filmed.*

Adult-facing:

> "I'm happy to appear in photos and video from the day." *Optional.*
